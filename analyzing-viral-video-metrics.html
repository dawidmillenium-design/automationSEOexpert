The four metrics that predict virality early

Full-history stats confirm what already happened; prediction requires leading indicators measured in the first 24 hours. Hook rate (viewers surviving three seconds) predicts whether the algorithm keeps testing your video. Average percentage viewed flags pacing problems before they cap reach. Share-rate-per-thousand-views is the single best proxy for the send-this-to-a-friend quality that drives exponential spread. Follow-through rate (profile visit after view) tells you whether traffic compounds into subscribers. Track these four per upload against your rolling median and you can usually classify a video's ceiling within a day.

Reading retention curves like an analyst

Retention graphs have grammar. A cliff at second one means a mismatch between promise and opening frame — fix packaging, not footage. A steady diagonal decline means pacing — tighten cuts or shorten duration. A dip at the midpoint followed by recovery signals a boring bridge section between hook and payoff — reorder segments. A spike near the end usually marks replay loops, which is gold: consider matching your final frame to your opening frame deliberately to amplify it. Train yourself to name the shape before prescribing the fix, because half of optimization advice fails by treating all drop-off as identical.
