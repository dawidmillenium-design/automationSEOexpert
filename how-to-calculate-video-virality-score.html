Worked example: scoring one video end to end

Take a clip with 40,000 views from a 5,000-follower account: 3,200 likes, 900 shares, 600 comments, average watch percentage 68%. Views-to-followers ratio is 8x, which alone clears the typical 2x threshold for out-of-pool performance. Shares per thousand views: 22.5 — strong, since most niches average under 10. Weighted naively (shares x3, comments x1.5, likes x1, completion x2), the composite lands roughly double the channel median: this video earned extended distribution because of the share mechanic, not luck. Record the components, not just the total — the breakdown is what tells you what to repeat.

Normalizing scores across platforms

A TikTok view and a YouTube view are not the same currency, so compare platforms on ratios, not absolutes: divide every interaction by views, then divide views by audience size. A 70% completion rate means roughly the same thing everywhere, and shares-per-thousand-views travels well too, while raw view counts do not. Keep one master sheet where each platform's videos are scored with the same normalized formula, and resist the temptation to tune weights per platform — the whole point of a composite score is comparability, and every extra free parameter makes your trend lines lie to you.
