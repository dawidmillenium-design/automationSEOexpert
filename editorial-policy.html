Editorial Policy & Methodology

This page defines the standards every article on Video Virality Score is held to. It exists so readers — and search engines evaluating our E-E-A-T signals — can see exactly how our advice is produced.

1. Sourcing standard

Claims must trace to one of four source tiers: (a) official platform documentation (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube help centres and engineering blogs); (b) our own instrumented campaigns with disclosed sample sizes; (c) peer-reviewed or industry-published research; (d) widely corroborated practitioner consensus — which we always label as such rather than as fact. Statements sourced only to tier (d) never carry numeric precision they cannot support.

2. How we test recommendations

Where possible we validate tactics directly: we run matched A/B tests on short-form uploads (varying hooks, captions, lengths or thumbnails) and compare hook rate, completion percentage and share rate over the first 72 hours. Guides state which recommendations come from tested experiments versus observational benchmarks.

3. Independence & disclosure

  • No affiliate links, sponsored placements or paid product recommendations appear inside editorial articles.
  • Tool round-ups include every leading option we can verify, ranked by published criteria, not commercial relationships.
  • If a future article ever involves a disclosed partnership, the disclosure sits above the fold and in the schema metadata.

4. Review process

Each guide is drafted by a named author (see credentials), fact-checked line-by-line against its cited sources by a second team member, and approved by the Head of Video Research before publication. Every page shows visible publication and update dates.

5. Refresh cycle

Platform algorithms change quarterly. Pillar guides are re-reviewed at least every six months; sections invalidated by platform changes are rewritten and the dateModified updated. Pages that fall below our depth standard get expanded or merged into a stronger guide with a canonical redirect of signals.

6. Corrections policy

Readers can report errors to team@example.com. Verified mistakes are fixed within five working days; material corrections are noted at the bottom of the affected article with the date and what changed. We do not silently rewrite substantively wrong claims.

7. AI assistance policy

Drafting may be assisted by AI tools, but every factual statement is verified by a human author, and no article is published without named-human review. Authorship, accountability and corrections always rest with the people listed on our authors page.
